Fleet Branding Audit · Vision AI

Partner fleets wrap their cars in branded or advertising vinyl, and keeping track of which car carries which design, and in what condition, used to mean people reviewing photos one by one. I built the system that does that review: photo capture with quality checks, an AI audit that compares every photo with its reference design, and one record per vehicle that the teams work from.

The problem

Photos arrived through a generic form and someone reviewed them by hand. There was no single record of which car carried which design, in what state, or when it was last checked, and the teams that depended on that information each kept their own version of it.

The fleet also changes every day: cars change drivers, go inactive or move between partners. Whatever I built had to follow the car itself, whoever took the photo.

The approach

  • Good photos first. The capture form guides the camera and checks size, brightness and sharpness in the browser before anything is uploaded. Most of the photos nobody can judge never get sent.
  • The model never judges a photo on its own. It sees the car next to the reference design for that vinyl and answers a fixed set of questions: which design, what condition, what damage. A fixed schema is what makes the answers countable and comparable over time.
  • People decide the unclear cases. When the model and the submission disagree, for example on the plate, the case goes to a person with both versions side by side instead of being guessed.
  • One record per vehicle. Every audit updates the same row for that car, so coverage, condition and follow-ups come from one place instead of several spreadsheets.

How it got here

This is the third version. The first one used models I trained myself; they were cheap but confused similar designs. The second moved to a vision LLM, which gave much richer answers about condition and damage but asked the model everything. The third put capture, audit, review and reporting into one circuit, and the hybrid vision engine later brought the repetitive checks back to small models.

The design held up to change. When a new mandatory sticker had to be checked, it went into the audit the same day: one more photo slot in the form and two more values in the model's answer, with no change to the data model.

Results

  • More than 20,000 photos audited, each with a stored verdict.
  • Above 95% agreement with human review. Most of the disagreements are photos too dark or blurred for anyone to judge, which is why the form now checks quality first.
  • Under an hour from photo to verdict, against days of manual review before.
  • Presented at a global all-hands in April 2026, and run by the local teams as business as usual since September.

What I would do differently

  • Build a labelled evaluation set on day one instead of tuning prompts against examples I already knew.
  • Move off spreadsheets as the database earlier. Under load they were the first thing to break.
