Events Intelligence

Forecasts learn from history, and history does not know that a stadium concert is on Saturday or that taxis strike on Thursday. Events Intelligence is a scheduled Claude skill that scans public sources for the week or month ahead across a dozen Spanish cities and hands operations a workbook they can plan from, hour by hour.

Why it exists

Forecasting models do well on a normal Tuesday and badly on the day of a derby, a trade fair or a strike, which are exactly the days that matter for staffing and supply. A scheduled skill now collects those days for every city, the same way every time.

How it works

  • Curated sources, grouped by type: league calendars, venue agendas, city listings, trade fair calendars, union and transport notices, port and airport schedules. All public.
  • Two windows. The weekly edition covers the next seven days; the monthly one covers the next calendar month and runs early enough to plan rosters.
  • Deduplication. The same concert appears on the venue site, the ticketing site and the city agenda. Each event gets a stable ID, so it is counted once and later runs update it instead of adding a copy.
  • Impact. Each event carries an attendance band, a time window with the build-up and the exit, and whether it moves demand or supply. A strike or a closure is flagged separately from a concert.
  • Delivery. A workbook with a master list, a day-by-hour grid and one tab per city, announced in Slack.

What I learned

  • A curated source list beats open search. It is boring to maintain and it is the reason the output is stable from one week to the next.
  • The exit matters as much as the start. Demand peaks when a stadium empties, not when the match begins, so every event carries its time window.
