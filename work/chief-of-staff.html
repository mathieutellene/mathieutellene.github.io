Personal AI Chief of Staff

Most of my day is context: what was decided in which thread, what a stakeholder asked for last week, where a number came from. I set up Claude as a chief of staff that holds that context for me: my projects, a knowledge folder it reads and maintains, skills for the work I repeat, and connectors to the tools I use. It works when I ask, and it asks before touching anything outside the conversation.

A knowledge folder with rules

A chat forgets. A folder does not. The knowledge folder holds stable facts about my role and projects, how I like things done, recurring workflows and a list of things never to do. The rules around it matter more than the content:

RuleWhat it means
Read everything firstEvery session starts by reading the whole knowledge folder and giving a short briefing.
Write what is learnedNew facts go to the right file during the conversation, with a one-line report at the end of each change.
Never overwrite a contradictionIf a new fact contradicts a stored one, it asks which is right instead of replacing it.
Never deleteAnything obsolete is moved to an archive folder with a date, never removed.
Ask before acting outwardSending a message, submitting a form or changing anything in a connected tool needs an explicit yes, every time.

What I use it for

  • Before a meeting: the last threads with those people, the open decisions and the numbers they will ask about.
  • After a meeting: the notes turned into tasks, and the facts that changed written back to the knowledge folder.
  • Documentation: the READMEs and handover documents for my projects start here, and I edit them.
  • Research: reading a dashboard, a set of documents or a Slack history and giving me the answer with its sources.

What I learned

  • Most of the value is in the context you give it and the rules you set, not in the model.
  • Asking before acting outward costs a second and prevents the one mistake you cannot undo.
